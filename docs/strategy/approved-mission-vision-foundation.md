# Approved Mission, Vision, and Foundation

Approved by James Akers on October 24, 2026 (America/Chicago).

These are the approved public-facing statements and the source of truth for future brand, website, biography, presentation, and partnership materials. Earlier long-form discovery statements remain supporting context; they do not supersede this wording.

## Mission

We help Pastors and Churches develop healthy servant leaders, equip people for ministry, and put their calling into practice through clear direction, shared responsibility, and meaningful service.

## Vision

We see Churches faithfully following Jesus, where Pastors are supported and people are equipped to carry the work of ministry together. These Churches know, love, and serve their neighbors, meet real needs, and share the gospel in their communities and around the world—while continuing to grow in the likeness of Christ.

## Foundation

Our work is biblically grounded, centered on Jesus Christ, and dependent on the Holy Spirit. We help leaders examine their practices in light of biblical truth, strengthen what is healthy, and make practical changes where needed.

## Governing rule for all project work

James’ directive, October 24, 2026: Everything done in this project must be assessed in light of the approved mission, vision, and foundation. Nothing we do or say may contradict them.

This requirement applies to all project decisions, internal and public language, resources, service design, relationships, partnerships, business practices, spending, branding, technology, and future expansion. It governs the work itself as well as its presentation.

Before recommending, approving, publishing, or putting work into practice, assess whether it:

1. Remains biblically grounded, centered on Jesus Christ, and dependent on the Holy Spirit.
2. Reflects servanthood, supports Pastors, equips people, and shares responsibility with appropriate care and support.
3. Helps Churches practice their calling, serve their neighbors, meet real needs, and share the gospel.
4. Strengthens what is healthy and addresses needed change truthfully, practically, and humbly.
5. Avoids claims, methods, relationships, or incentives that undermine these commitments.

Not every item must repeat every statement or serve every purpose equally, but nothing may contradict them. If a conflict is found, name it clearly and revise or set aside the conflicting proposal before proceeding. Do not silently weaken or rewrite the approved statements to accommodate an idea. Bring any proposed change to the statements to James explicitly.

Existing materials remain subject to this standard; recording the rule does not mean a retrospective assessment has already been completed.

## Decisions carried forward

- Preserve the capitalization of **Pastors** and **Churches** in these statements and future public-facing brand copy.
- Servanthood and servant leadership are central commitments, including knowing, loving, and serving neighbors.
- Use clear, practical, biblical, truth-driven language. The public-facing foundation replaces the earlier opening “With Scripture open.”
- The mission describes our contribution; the vision describes the future we hope to see; the foundation states what governs the work. These are ministry statements, not Scripture quotations or guarantees of outcomes.
- Equipping includes formation, appropriate authority, resources, and ongoing support. Shared responsibility must not become unsupported delegation.
- Prayer with Pastors and staff is not a ceremonial opening or closing. It belongs before evaluation, when difficult truths surface, and before deciding the next step. Together we come before the Father, listen to Jesus through His Word, and ask the Holy Spirit to lead. We can support faithful response, but God is the One who produces real life change.
- **Titus 2:11–15 is formative for this work.** Paul tells Titus to teach and encourage the Church around the life-giving grace revealed in Jesus: grace that brings salvation, forms the way we live, and produces a people who belong to Christ and are eager to do good. This keeps encouragement, formation, practical obedience, and clear Christ-centered teaching together.
- Pastors and Churches remain the current audience. Adaptation for Christ-centered nonprofits is a future expansion, not current service development. Leave room for it when choosing the name and brand.
- The three requested editorial perspectives reviewed the statements: Protestant Christian public relations, Church leadership/executive pastoral ministry, and publicity. James approved the resulting wording and requested recording it in GitHub.
