# The Posture of the Work

## Approved Working Statement

I will not walk into a church assuming I already know what God is saying to its leaders. I will bring experience, questions, an outside perspective, and a willingness to name what may be difficult to see from the inside. The pastor and leadership team bring their calling, history, relationships, and knowledge of their people. Together, with Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, we will listen for where the Lord is directing us.

That means asking honest questions, bringing hidden costs and unhealthy systems into the light, and challenging practices that have become normal but may not reflect the life, teachings, character, or methods of Jesus.

This is not about comparing pastors or churches to the Pharisees and Sadducees. It is about recognizing a temptation common to all of us: we can build systems that seem right, become comfortable with them, and eventually defend them without stopping to ask whether they still reflect the heart of Christ.

Jesus was willing to challenge human traditions when they displaced God's Word, burdened people, protected appearances, or lost sight of mercy, justice, and faithfulness. We must be willing to examine our systems with that same honesty.

The goal is not to replace one human system with another. It is to discern together what the Holy Spirit is revealing, strengthen what is life giving, bring what is hidden into the light, and build systems that make room for the life Jesus is already growing.

## What This Posture Requires

### Humility

The advisor does not enter as the person who has heard from God on behalf of everyone else. Experience and an outside perspective matter, but they do not replace the pastor's calling, the wisdom present in the body, Scripture, prayer, or the leading of the Holy Spirit.

### Courage

Humility does not mean avoiding what is difficult. The work includes asking questions people may have learned not to ask, naming hidden costs, and helping leaders look honestly at systems that have become untouchable.

### Shared discernment

The advisor, pastor, staff, and appropriate spiritual leadership listen together. They bring testimony, history, Scripture, prayer, disagreement, and experience into the conversation. Agreement is not manufactured, and one strong personality is not allowed to impersonate the voice of the Holy Spirit.

### Biblical examination

Familiarity does not make a practice biblical. Longevity does not make it healthy. Success does not make it faithful. Every system remains open to examination through Scripture and the life, character, priorities, and methods of Jesus.

### Pastoral care

Bringing something into the light should serve truth, healing, responsibility, and faithful change. It should not humiliate a pastor, create suspicion for its own sake, or use an outsider's perspective as a weapon.

### Practical obedience

Discernment should eventually lead to a faithful response. The response may be to strengthen, share, simplify, redesign, pause, repent, repair, or release. The next step should be clear enough to practice and small enough to evaluate honestly.

## Biblical Anchors

### Acts 15:1–9, 12–22, 28–29 — The Holy Spirit and shared discernment

The Jerusalem council did not begin with an expert arriving with an answer. There was sharp disagreement, a gathered leadership body, substantial discussion, testimony about what God had done, examination of Scripture, a judgment offered by James, agreement among the leaders and church, and a clear communication sent back to the people affected.

Their conclusion could be described as fitting both the Holy Spirit's leading and their shared discernment. This is not a formula suggesting that every group decision automatically speaks for the Spirit. It is a picture of leaders listening, testing, and deciding together under God.

### John 16:12–15 — The Spirit guides and glorifies Jesus

Jesus promises that the Spirit of truth will guide His people. The Spirit's work does not draw attention away from Jesus; it glorifies Him. A discernment process should therefore become more clearly centered on Christ, not merely more confident in the opinions already present in the room.

### Mark 7:6–13 — Tradition must not displace God's Word

Jesus confronts religious leaders for allowing human traditions to override God's commands. The problem is not structure or tradition by itself. The problem is a system becoming protected even when it works against what God has made clear.

### Matthew 23:1–12, 23–28 — Systems can burden people and protect appearances

Jesus names leaders who place heavy loads on others without helping carry them. He also confronts meticulous religious performance that neglects justice, mercy, and faithfulness, as well as outward appearances that hide an unhealthy interior life.

This gives the work permission to ask who is carrying the weight, whether leaders practice what they ask of others, and whether visible activity is hiding an inward problem.

### Mark 2:23–28 — People are not made to serve religious machinery

Jesus' teaching about the Sabbath reminds us that God's gifts and commands are not given to crush the people receiving them. This passage should not be reduced to a general statement about organizational systems, but it does reinforce an important question: has something intended to serve life become a burden people now exist to maintain?

## Guardrails

- We do not assume disagreement with a system proves that the system is unbiblical.
- We do not use “the Holy Spirit told me” to shut down questions, Scripture, wisdom, or accountability.
- We do not compare a pastor or church casually to the Pharisees and Sadducees.
- We acknowledge that all of us can protect familiar systems, including the advisor.
- We distinguish practices that are merely ineffective from practices that are unhealthy, harmful, or unfaithful.
- We do not expose something simply to prove that we saw it. We bring it into the light so truth, healing, responsibility, and faithful change can begin.
- We do not replace one rigid template with another.
- We return decisions to prayer, Scripture, appropriate leadership, and the people responsible for carrying them.

## A Simple Way to Explain It

> I am not coming in with a blueprint for your church. I am coming with experience, questions, an outside perspective, and the courage to help us look honestly at what may be difficult to see from the inside. You know your calling, your people, and your history. Together, with Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, we will listen for where the Lord is directing us and build a faithful next step from there.

## Questions to Carry Into Every Engagement

1. What are we assuming before we have listened?
2. Whose voice or experience is missing from the conversation?
3. What are people afraid to bring into the light?
4. What practice has become untouchable, and why?
5. Does this practice reflect the words, character, priorities, and methods of Jesus?
6. Who benefits from the current system?
7. Who carries its hidden cost?
8. Are we looking for the Spirit's direction or asking God to endorse a decision already made?
9. What is life giving here?
10. What is the next faithful step we can practice and evaluate together?
