# Calling, Vision, Mission, and Life-Giving Systems

## Working Discovery Guide

This guide is meant to help us move from a burden we feel to a calling we can explain, a future we can picture, a mission we can practice, and systems that help people carry it together.

We will use it on this endeavor first. Once we have worked through it honestly and tested the questions, we can adapt it into a guide for pastors, staff teams, and church boards.

This is not an exercise in finding impressive words. The goal is clarity that can guide real decisions.

## Christ at the Center

Before we ask what we want to build, we begin with Jesus.

- What do the life, teachings, character, and methods of Jesus show us about this work?
- What did Jesus consistently move toward?
- Who did He notice, welcome, challenge, form, entrust, restore, and send?
- Where did He refuse to let urgency, popularity, opposition, or expectation determine His direction?
- What has Jesus already made clear that we do not need to reinvent?
- Where do we need prayer, patience, and the leading of the Holy Spirit rather than another planning session?
- What would make this work merely Christ-adjacent instead of truly Christ-centered?

> **Jesus remains the standard—not church trends, organizational growth, attendance, branding, efficiency, or financial success.**

### Primary Scripture for this endeavor

**2 Timothy 1:6–7** is the primary Scripture gathering this work together. It holds gift, calling, spiritual empowerment, love, and sound judgment in the same place. The supporting passages and the guardrail against using “fan into flame” as pressure on an exhausted pastor are recorded in `docs/strategy/scriptural-foundation.md`.

## At a Glance

| Part | What it helps us understand | The central question | What it should produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calling and Purpose** | Why this work has been entrusted to us | Why are we here, and what part of Christ's heart are we called to carry? | A clear reason for existing that can guide decisions over time |
| **Vision** | The future we are prayerfully moving toward | If this work becomes healthier and more faithful, what will be different for the people and places we serve? | A future people can picture and move toward together |
| **Mission** | What we are committed to doing now | Who are we serving, what are we helping them do, and how will we serve them? | A plain-language description of the work we actually do |
| **Systems** | How the mission becomes repeatable and shared | What rhythms, responsibilities, tools, and boundaries will help us practice the mission faithfully? | A workable way of carrying the mission without depending on one person to hold everything |
| **Life-Giving Reach** | Whether the work is receiving and sharing life in every direction | Is the life of Jesus being received here, growing here, and reaching beyond here? | Honest evidence of health, cost, fruit, and the next faithful adjustment |

The movement is:

**Calling gives us the reason. Vision gives us a picture. Mission gives us direction. Systems give the work a faithful rhythm. The Life-Giving Reach Test helps us see what those systems are producing and costing.**

## Part One: Calling and Purpose

Calling and purpose answer why this work exists. For a church, the foundation is received from Christ rather than invented by a leadership team. The local question is how that shared biblical calling is being expressed through these people, in this place, during this season.

### Questions to work through

- What burden keeps returning, even when we try to set it aside?
- Who or what has God consistently drawn our attention toward?
- What experiences, gifts, relationships, and lessons have prepared us to serve here?
- What need are we willing to stay present with after the excitement of starting has passed?
- What change do we hope people experience because this work exists?
- What part of the character and ministry of Jesus should people encounter through us?
- What would be missing from the people or community we serve if this work were absent?
- What responsibility has God given us that we cannot faithfully hand to someone else?
- What good work matters but is not ours to carry?

### Working statement

> We believe God has entrusted us to ____________________________________________
> for ______________________________________________________________________
> so that ___________________________________________________________________.

### Clarity check

- Does this begin with service and faithfulness rather than our own recognition?
- Is it rooted in something already true, or are we trying to manufacture an identity?
- Is it specific enough to help us say both yes and no?
- Could we still recognize this calling if the size, setting, or delivery method changed?

## Part Two: Vision

Vision gives people a picture of a faithful future. It is not a guarantee about attendance, money, influence, or visible success. It describes what we prayerfully hope will become true as we participate in what God is doing.

### Questions to work through

- If this work becomes healthier and more faithful over the next three to five years, what will be different?
- What will pastors be able to carry differently?
- What will people inside the church understand, experience, or participate in differently?
- What will the surrounding community experience differently?
- How could this local work contribute to what Christ is doing beyond the community and around the world?
- What relationships will exist that do not exist today?
- What will leaders stop carrying alone?
- What will we be trusted for?
- What might faithfulness look like even if growth is slower or less visible than expected?

### Working statement

> We see ____________________________________________________________________
> where _____________________________________________________________________
> and where __________________________________________________________________.

### Clarity check

- Can people picture it?
- Does it grow naturally from the calling?
- Is it hopeful without promising outcomes only God can produce?
- Does it describe people and changed realities rather than our own prominence?

## Part Three: Mission

Mission describes what we do now. It gives the calling and vision hands and feet. A mission should be clear enough that a pastor, staff member, board member, or volunteer can recognize how the work in front of them connects to it.

### Questions to work through

- Who are we specifically called and equipped to serve?
- What are we helping them understand, change, build, or carry?
- What do we actually do with them—not merely for them?
- How will our approach reflect the character and methods of Jesus?
- What makes our contribution different from resources they can already find elsewhere?
- What must remain relational and cannot be reduced to a product or process?
- What work will we not do, even if someone is willing to pay for it?
- What should someone see us doing on an ordinary Monday that proves this mission is real?

### Working statement

> We serve __________________________________________________________________
> by ________________________________________________________________________
> so they can _________________________________________________________________
> while ______________________________________________________________________.

### Clarity check

- Is it written in plain language?
- Does it identify who we serve and what we do?
- Can someone connect it to our actual calendar, budget, and responsibilities?
- Does it help us make tradeoffs?
- Is it short enough to remember without becoming so broad that it could belong to anyone?

## Part Four: Systems

Systems are the repeatable rhythms, responsibilities, tools, and boundaries that help people practice the mission together. They should serve the calling without becoming a substitute for prayer, discernment, relationships, or dependence on the Holy Spirit.

> **Systems do not create life. Jesus does. Healthy systems make room for the life He is already growing.**

### Questions to work through

- What must happen consistently for the mission to become real?
- What work currently depends on one person remembering, carrying, or rescuing it?
- Who should own each responsibility, and who should share the weight?
- What needs a simple process, checklist, calendar, or follow-up rhythm?
- Where do people need formation before they are given responsibility?
- What authority, information, resources, and support must travel with an assignment?
- Where should people return to reflect, learn, receive correction, celebrate, and rest?
- What boundaries will keep the system from consuming the people it was meant to serve?
- What should be strengthened, simplified, redesigned, paused, or released?
- How often will we stop and ask whether the system is still serving its purpose?

## Part Five: The Life-Giving Reach Test

Christ is the source of life. This test helps us examine whether a system is making room for that life to be received, formed, and shared through the pastor, the congregation, the community, and the world.

Life-giving does not mean easy, painless, or personally enjoyable. Following Jesus can be costly. But faithful sacrifice is different from chronic depletion caused by isolation, confusion, poor stewardship, or systems that continually take without forming, supporting, or bearing fruit.

### The four areas

| Area | The life-giving question |
|---|---|
| **A. The Pastor** | Is this helping the pastor remain rooted, healthy, supported, and faithful—or is it quietly consuming the person carrying it? |
| **B. The Inside Congregation** | Is this forming, equipping, connecting, and releasing people into ministry—or merely keeping them busy? |
| **C. The Outside Community** | Is this helping the church listen, build trust, serve real people, and bear faithful witness beyond its walls? |
| **D. The World Through Missions** | Is this cultivating prayer, generosity, partnership, sending, and concern for what Christ is doing beyond the local community? |

### Guardrail

Not every individual ministry must serve all four areas equally. A grief ministry, children's ministry, local outreach, or missionary partnership may have a particular focus. We should ask whether each ministry is healthy within its calling and whether the whole ministry life of the church reaches all four areas over time.

The question running through every area is:

> **Is the life of Jesus being received here, growing here, and reaching beyond here?**

## Final Integrity Tests

### The Jesus test

Does the work itself reflect the character, priorities, and methods of Jesus—or have we attached spiritual language to a conventional plan?

### The plain-language test

Could a pastor, board member, volunteer, and person outside the church explain what we mean without needing the original planning team in the room?

### The decision test

Can these statements help us decide what to begin, what to strengthen, what to decline, and what to release?

### The ownership test

Does the mission belong to the whole body, with real responsibility and support shared among people, or does it still depend on one leader carrying it?

### The Life-Giving Reach Test

Is the life of Jesus being received, growing, and reaching outward through the pastor, the inside congregation, the outside community, and the world through missions?

## Working Outcome

At the end of this process, we should have:

1. A clear calling and purpose statement
2. A vision people can picture
3. A mission people can practice
4. The first systems needed to carry it
5. An honest view of who is receiving life, who is carrying the cost, and where change is needed
6. A small next step and a date to evaluate what we are learning

The goal is not better wording by itself. The goal is a healthier, more faithful way of living what Christ has entrusted to us.
