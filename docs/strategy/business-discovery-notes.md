# The Calling

I want to come alongside pastors, staff, and church boards who need an experienced outside set of eyes.

The work may involve ministry focus, leadership health, community engagement, systems, technology, healthy AI use, marketing, first impressions, branding, vision, or mission. The service may change from church to church. The reason for doing it will not.

## Who I want to serve

This is less about the size of a church and more about where that church finds itself.

I am thinking about the church that keeps hitting the same ceiling. It may have reached a plateau and cannot seem to move beyond it. It wants to stay connected to the community, but it struggles to sustain those relationships. Its people are passionate about life change and reaching others for Christ, but they cannot seem to keep traction. Its pastor may be showing signs of burnout.

It is a:

> mission-driven church whose passion has outgrown its systems

## Why this work is personal

I have served as a Children's Pastor, Youth Pastor, College Pastor, Outreach Pastor, and Discipleship Pastor. I have also worked in IT leadership and sales management.

I am the son of a pastor. I cannot remember a time when I was not in church. I have seen the good, the bad, and the ugly from the inside.

I have helped launch churches that met in schools and recreation centers. I have served in megachurches. I have watched churches close their doors.

That history matters. I understand ministry from more than one seat. I know the strain leaders carry. I also know that systems, technology, and clear communication can either support the calling or slowly drain the life out of it.

The voice of this work must sound like me: charismatic, pastoral, warm, direct, conviction-filled, hopeful, conversational, and grounded in Scripture. It needs to be:

> more charismatic and less stiff

## The Heartbeat

> **Everything we create must be Christ-centered—not merely Christ-adjacent.** We will consistently examine the life, teachings, character, and methods of Jesus. We will consider His intentionality, His compassion, His directness, His specific instructions, the questions He asked, the relationships He formed, and the way He equipped, entrusted, sent, corrected, encouraged, restored, and multiplied people.
>
> We will not simply attach Scripture to conventional business practices. We will ask whether the practice itself reflects the character and mission of Christ.
>
> Jesus will remain the standard—not church trends, organizational growth, attendance, branding, efficiency, or financial success.

This does not mean forcing a Gospel story onto every process or pretending that every moment in Jesus' ministry is an organizational formula. It means letting His life and teaching question our methods before we ask Him to bless them.

Every part of the work should bring us back to a few direct questions:

- Where do we see this in the life or teaching of Jesus?
- Does it carry both His grace and His truth?
- Is it forming people and preparing them for ministry?
- Does it protect what must remain sacred and deeply human?
- Is it producing real life, discipleship, service, faithfulness, and multiplication?
- Are we following Jesus, or are we using His name to validate what we already wanted to do?

## What “better” means

Better does not mean bigger, busier, richer, or more impressive. It means healthier.

We are helping cultivate a healthy church—rooted in Christ, alive through the Holy Spirit, tended by the Father, and bearing fruit that reaches people with the saving grace of Jesus.

The primary Scripture for this endeavor is **2 Timothy 1:6–7**. Paul reminds Timothy to tend the gift God has already placed in him, and he locates faithful ministry in the Spirit's power, love, and sound judgment rather than fear. This must never be flattened into telling an exhausted pastor to try harder. The wider foundation is recorded in `docs/strategy/scriptural-foundation.md`.

The approved **Posture of the Work** is saved in `docs/strategy/posture-of-the-work.md`. James will not enter a church pretending to have God's answer for its leaders. He will bring experience, questions, an outside perspective, and the courage to help bring difficult things into the light. The pastor and leadership team bring their calling, history, relationships, and knowledge of their people. Together, with Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, they will listen, discern, and build a faithful next step.

Jesus is the vine. Apart from Him, we can do nothing. The Holy Spirit is the living water who brings life from within. The Father is the gardener who tends the branches and prepares them to bear more fruit. Good soil receives the Word and allows it to take root.

Biblical anchors: John 15:1–5; John 7:37–39; Matthew 13:23.

## The 1–3–12 leadership pattern

A pastor nearing burnout may be doing nearly everything alone. The 1–3–12 pattern helps us look at self-leadership, trusted relationships, discipleship, and shared ministry.

It comes from looking closely at Jesus' relationships with Peter, James, John, and the Twelve. It is a principle, not a command about staffing levels. It is not a rigid organizational chart or a promise that a church will grow.

The clearest expression of the pattern is:

> **Lead from a life centered under Christ. Share life and truth deeply with a trusted few. Form a broader circle of close disciple-leaders—including that trusted few—and entrust them with real ministry. Send in companionship, receive them back for reflection and rest, and depend on the Holy Spirit for fruit and multiplication.**

Christ is at the center. The circles are nested:

- **1 — Stewardship:** Lead yourself under Christ. Care for your spiritual life, emotional health, physical capacity, priorities, boundaries, and calling.
- **3 — Trusted proximity:** Know the people who can carry weight with you, tell you the truth, encourage you, and help lift your arms when your strength is failing.
- **12 — Shared formation:** Build a wider circle of close disciples and capable leaders. The three remain part of the twelve. Teach them, trust them, give them real ministry, and help them grow.

Moses, Aaron, and Hur give us a supporting picture of people helping carry a leader when his arms could no longer stay raised (Exodus 17:12). Jesus gives us the relational pattern of the Three within the Twelve.

The point is not simply to hand out more tasks.

> The same Holy Spirit that is gifting you, empowering you, teaching you is in them and calling them

The deeper revelation is that Jesus loved everyone fully, but He did not give everyone the same access, assignment, or responsibility. Different proximity did not mean different value.

Barna's findings show why this matters. In 2022, 80 percent of pastors said someone outside their home and church knew them well, yet 65 percent reported feeling lonely or isolated at least sometimes. Only 22 percent received spiritual support from peers or a mentor several times a month or more. These are separate measures, not one scale, but they reveal an important tension:

> **Being known is not always the same as being supported. Being surrounded is not the same as being carried.**

The Three were present when Jesus confronted death at Jairus's house (Mark 5:37), revealed His glory on the mountain (Mark 9:2), and carried sorrow in Gethsemane (Mark 14:33). Peter, James, and John were not simply His “top leaders.” They were trusted with moments the crowd did not see.

This reframes the circles:

- **Christ — The source:** Christ is not another responsibility. He holds the leader and the ministry.
- **1 — The soul beneath the role:** Before anyone sees the pastor, Christ knows the person.
- **3 — The few who know the cost:** They see more than the platform. They witness both the fruit and the cost.
- **12 — People formed to carry the mission:** They are not merely helpers. They are brought close, taught the heart, given real responsibility, and sent.

> **The crowd may see what the pastor carries. The trusted few must be allowed to see what it costs.**

> **Different proximity is not favoritism. It is faithful stewardship.**

The continuing rhythm is:

**Abide → Share Life → Form → Entrust → Send Together → Return, Reflect, and Rest → Multiply through the Spirit.**

Questions worth asking:

- **1:** Am I leading and caring for myself well under Christ?
- **3:** Who knows me, tells me the truth, shares the weight, and helps lift my arms?
- **12:** Who is ready to be formed, trusted, and given meaningful responsibility—including the three?
- What gifts are already present in these people?
- Where do they need to grow?
- What can I safely and meaningfully trust them to own?

## The formation gap

Most pastors already believe ministry should be shared. The deeper question is why so many still find themselves carrying it alone.

Barna's research helps reveal the distance between conviction and practice: 96 percent of Protestant senior pastors surveyed in 2019 agreed that laypeople taking responsibility for the work of the church is a mark of health, while 41 percent said their church did a poor job developing new leaders. Only 9 percent strongly agreed that their team did this well. In 2026, pastors also placed delegation among both the most helpful and the most difficult steps toward burnout relief.

Those findings are not a verdict against exhausted pastors. They help us name a formation problem:

> **If the first time we think about delegation is when the pastor is exhausted, we waited too long.**

Jesus did not merely hand out tasks when the work became heavy. He called the Twelve to be with Him before He sent them out. They were close enough to watch, ask, fail, and learn. He gave them authority and a clear assignment. He sent them together. When they returned, He received their report and called them to rest.

The rhythm is:

**With Him → Sent Together → Back With Him**

The question is not only, “What can I get off my plate?” It is:

> **Who is close enough to watch you lead, understand why, carry something real, and come back to talk about it?**

The first step does not require fixing the whole system. Start with one person already near you. Invite that person into one real part of ministry.

## From scattered passion to focused impact

Most people live like flashlights. Their energy spreads wide and thin across too many activities, concerns, and obstacles. I want to help them become more like a laser. The difference is focus.

> (Passion + Vision x Mission = Extravagant Results)

> (Passion - Vision / Mission = No Impact)

> **Passion is powerful. But without vision to focus it and mission to move it, passion becomes scattered energy.**

> **Passion creates the energy. Vision brings it into focus. Mission points it somewhere that matters. Impact happens where it lands.**

The picture is simple:

- **Passion** is the light.
- **Vision** is the lens that brings the light into focus.
- **Mission** is the surface that points it somewhere that matters.
- **Impact** is what happens where the light lands.

The lens remains whole, but a softly clouded center shows unclear vision without calling the church broken. Several mission surfaces show divided direction pulling the church's energy different ways.

Unclear vision leaves the energy spread wide. Divided direction pulls the work different ways.

In the second path, the passion is still present, but unclear vision leaves it spread wide and divided direction means less reaches where it was meant to land.

**Limited Impact** does not mean small attendance, a small church, or insignificant ministry. A small, faithful ministry can carry deep, life-giving fruit. Here, limited means that less of the church's available energy is reaching its particular God-given destination.

The equations are memorable pictures, not guaranteed spiritual outcomes. We align what we carry with where Jesus is leading. Only Jesus gives life.

## When the light grows dim

Burnout is not the same thing as scattered passion. It needs its own conversation.

> **Misalignment scatters passion. Burnout drains the capacity to carry it.**

> **Some pastors are not losing their passion. They are losing the capacity to carry it.**

Jesus received the disciples when they returned. He let them report what had happened. He called them away from the crowd to rest. He withdrew to pray. He allowed trusted friends to see His sorrow. He formed people and shared ministry with them.

That pattern gives us a starting place for care, honest conversation, shared responsibility, and healing. The complete work is preserved in `docs/strategy/when-the-light-grows-dim-framework.md`.

## Build the foundation first

Before I help other pastors and churches define their mission and vision, I need to establish the mission and vision for this work. That is not starting over. It is putting a foundation beneath what has already begun.

Jesus called the disciples to be with Him before He sent them out (Mark 3:13–15). Presence came before assignment. Formation came before activity.

The order for this work should be:

1. Central Scriptures and theological foundation
2. Mission
3. Vision
4. Voice and visual brand

The brand will include colors, typography, a logo, an email signature, and a consistent visual direction. It should reveal what is already true at the center. It should never become image management or a substitute for substance.

## The working mission

> With Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, we walk alongside pastors and churches to ask honest questions, bring unhealthy systems into the light, and build life-giving practices that help them carry their calling with clarity.
>
> We help pastors lead from renewed strength, equip people to share the work of ministry, understand the communities around them, and take faithful, sustainable steps toward serving people who are hungry, thirsty, unhoused, addicted, incarcerated, abused, orphaned, or widowed—locally, nationally, and around the world. When it serves the church and its calling, this may include connecting leaders with trusted resources and ministry partners already doing the work well.
>
> Our role is not to arrive with every answer or recreate every ministry. It is to help churches recognize their calling, prepare their people, build healthy systems, and follow where the Lord is directing them.

This defines our lane. We are an advisor, facilitator, connector, and encourager. We do not replace the local church, and we do not try to duplicate every ministry a church may need. We help leaders recognize what God has entrusted to them, build the systems and relationships needed to carry it, and take the next faithful step.

> **Churches should leave with relationships, not merely recommendations.**

This mission is approved as a working foundation. It can be shortened and refined for public use after the vision is clear, but its meaning should not be thinned out.

### Relationships that grow from the work

We are not starting by building a network. We are starting by faithfully serving the pastor and church in front of us.

As the work grows, relationships will grow with it. Churches may discover trusted ministry partners, pastors may find other leaders who understand what they are carrying, and healthy connections may begin forming across communities.

If God allows those relationships to become a larger ecosystem, we will steward it faithfully. But the network is not the product, the requirement, or the center of the work. It is something that may grow naturally from serving people well.

A church can receive honest counsel, practical tools, and meaningful help without joining anything. Connection is available when it gives life; it is never the price of receiving help.

## Systems should give life

I want to help pastors and staff use systems without becoming victims of those systems.

Every ministry, meeting, process, activity, and tool should be able to face one simple question:

> Is it life giving?

Some systems create room for people to serve, grow, breathe, and carry ministry together. Others consume time and energy long after anyone remembers why they began. We need the courage to know the difference.

Jesus was intentional. He knew when to teach the crowd, when to step away, when to speak to the Twelve, and when to bring the Three closer. His methods served the mission. The mission never became a servant of the method.

## A picture of renewal

The tree begins bare, but it is not dead. The roots still hold life. Buds appear. Leaves and fruit follow. Seeds take root in the ground nearby. What began as renewal in one tree becomes life around it.

The movement is:

1. **Rooted:** What looks dormant may still be deeply rooted in Christ. The roots may be holding more life than anyone can see.
2. **Awakening:** Clarity gives that buried passion somewhere to go. Vision sharpens. Mission becomes clear. New life starts breaking through.
3. **Flourishing:** The church is no longer spending all its strength just surviving. Healthy systems make room for people to grow, serve, and breathe.
4. **Fruitful:** The church moves beyond its walls. Trust grows. Relationships deepen. The community begins to experience the fruit.
5. **Multiplying:** Life no longer stops at the original tree. Seeds take root. New leaders rise. New ministry grows. The impact reaches beyond a program, a building, or one personality.

## From diagnosis to practice

> **We have learned how to name what is happening. Now we need to help churches know what to do next.**

We have done meaningful work to identify issues, but we have not yet built the systems churches can put in place to address them. Diagnosis is necessary, but it cannot be the finish line.

Every framework should move through the same questions:

1. What are we seeing?
2. Why might it be happening?
3. What does Jesus show us?
4. What life-giving system could address it?
5. How will the church practice, evaluate, and refine it?

The working rhythm is **Listen → Discern → Design → Practice → Evaluate → Refine**. The full systems roadmap is saved in `OPEN-WORK-AND-NEXT-STEPS.md`.
