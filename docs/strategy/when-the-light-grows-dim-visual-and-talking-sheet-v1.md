### When the Light Grows Dim

**Some Pastors are not losing their passion. They are losing the capacity to carry it.**

You can still love Jesus, love people, and believe deeply in your calling while being worn down by what you've carried.

Some things don't end when Sunday is over. The grief comes home. The conflict follows you. You find yourself bracing for the next call.

### The Heartbeat

When the disciples returned, Jesus heard what they had done and taught. They were so pressed they had not had time to eat. He invited them to come away and rest.

Mark 6:30-32, paraphrase.

### Make room to recover.

Protect rest with someone else actually covering the work.

### Bring care close.

Make room for trusted support and qualified professional care. Receiving care is not spiritual failure.

### Change what keeps causing harm.

Share responsibility and change the expectations that keep consuming the Pastor.

**Do not send a wounded Pastor back into an unchanged fire.**

Short care note for eventual layout: This is a conversation about care, not a diagnosis or treatment plan.

The Mark paraphrase is based on [Mark 6:30-34, NKJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+6%3A30-34&version=NKJV). The final PDF should display the passage reference, not a clickable link.

## Talking sheet: opening the conversation

### Begin here

"Pastor, before we talk about what the church needs to do next, I want to make room for what you've been carrying.

"There may be things you have walked through that never had room to settle. Sunday came again. Someone else needed you. You kept going.

"I don't want to assume I know what that has cost you. You don't have to explain it in this room. We can make room for a private conversation with someone you trust. And the church can begin sharing the weight without asking you to tell every part of your story."

Pause. Do not fill the silence or turn it into a demand for disclosure. People may decline the conversation. Prayer may be offered with permission, not required as a test of openness.

### The Heartbeat

Read Mark 6:30-34 together. The apostles reported back; Jesus noticed their lack of time even to eat and invited them to rest. The crowd then arrived, and He responded with compassion. Preserve both parts: He cared about the disciples and the people they served. The passage is not a clinical recovery plan or proof that every demand disappears when we choose rest. Our application is that caring for people includes caring for those carrying ministry. [Mark 6:30-34, NKJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+6%3A30-34&version=NKJV).

In Gethsemane Jesus also voiced His sorrow and asked trusted disciples to remain near. We point to His honesty and dependence on the Father, not claim He had burnout or PTSD. [Matthew 26:36-46, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+26%3A36-46&version=NIV).

Our primary foundation remains 2 Timothy 1:6-7, read with verse 8: God's gift, His power, love, and self-discipline in costly ministry. Do not use the flame language to tell an exhausted Pastor to push harder or treat distress as evidence of a deficient Spirit. That is our pastoral application, not a medical promise in the passage. [2 Timothy 1:6-8, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+1%3A6-8&version=NIV).

### Questions for the church's leaders

- What have we learned to call normal that keeps asking too much of the Pastor?
- If the Pastor stepped away to receive care, who would actually carry the work?
- What can we remove or share now, rather than wait for a crisis?
- Are we making it safe to ask for help, or rewarding the appearance of never needing it?

These address the church's practices. Do not ask a Pastor to describe traumatic events, symptoms, or treatment in a board meeting or presentation. If a leader or board has contributed to harm, use an independent safe person and appropriate professional support rather than making that same setting the required place for disclosure.

### Questions offered privately, with permission

"Would it help to talk privately about what ministry has been costing you?"

If welcomed: "What would help you feel supported right now? Who would you feel safe receiving help from?"

Follow the Pastor's lead. Explain confidentiality and its safety/reporting limits before sensitive conversation; do not promise absolute secrecy. Do not take a trauma history, investigate memories, or try to process trauma as an advisor. A Pastor's private care information is not an appendix to the church's action plan.

### A first response the church can own

Agree on one immediate relief the Pastor welcomes: remove a nonessential demand, arrange real backup coverage, or help make an agreed professional-care connection. Name who will arrange it, when it begins, and how interruptions will be handled. Confirm the covering Staff/Volunteers have willingness, capacity, time, and support; do not transfer the overload to another person.

Record operational changes, not sensitive disclosures. Review whether the promised relief actually happened. Longer leave, role changes, treatment, and return decisions require individualized discernment and relevant professional guidance; this first response is not a complete recovery plan.

### Language and care boundaries

Burnout and PTSD are not interchangeable. WHO describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition. It does not establish PTSD or rule out another condition. [WHO: Burn-out](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon).

Persistent distress, disrupted sleep, intrusive memories, avoidance, or feeling continually on alert deserve appropriate attention; these are not a diagnosis, a checklist, or unique to PTSD. Use "trauma-related stress symptoms" only where traumatic exposure is relevant; otherwise use ordinary descriptions of distress. A qualified mental-health provider assesses and diagnoses PTSD. Encourage appropriate medical or mental-health care when distress is significant or interferes with daily life. [VA: PTSD Basics](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/what/ptsd_basics.asp).

Do not describe this as a quick reset, imply more prayer replaces needed care, require testimony, equate productivity with healing, or let the church's need for coverage set a recovery deadline.

If there is suicide risk or an urgent emotional crisis in the United States, call or text 988; for immediate danger call 911. Stop the advisory discussion and help connect with appropriate urgent support. [988 Lifeline: What to Expect](https://988lifeline.org/get-help/what-to-expect/).

### Transition when appropriate

"The next conversation is about who can come close and help carry the weight. Before we move there, let's make sure care and actual relief are in place."

Do not move through the book on schedule if someone needs the conversation to stop. The remaining chapters are not prerequisites for receiving care.
