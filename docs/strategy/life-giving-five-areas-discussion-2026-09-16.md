## James' proposed refinement

Preserve "Is this life giving?" as a central question for evaluating every ministry. Examine five groups separately:

1. Pastor
2. Staff
3. Local Church
4. Community
5. World (Missions)

James proposes that each ministry must be life giving to a minimum of three of the five. He asked us to think through whether and how this belongs in the questions PDF. This is a proposal under discussion, not an agreed replacement for the four-area guardrail in the existing draft. The questions PDF has not been revised yet.

## James' example: Community Food Pantry

| Group | James' proposed evaluation and reason |
|---|---|
| Pastor | No, unless the Pastor is the only staff member. |
| Staff | Yes. Creates opportunities for Staff to show leadership within the local church, extend to other local church leadership or laity, and expand social and professional networks. |
| Local Church | Yes. Creates opportunities for people who might not otherwise have a chance to serve. |
| Community | Yes. Meets a physical need and creates opportunities to meet spiritual needs. |
| World (Missions) | No, unless intentionally raising funds for missions or raising awareness. |

The example illustrates the intended three-of-five threshold. These are illustrative classifications, not findings from an actual church assessment. Spiritual conversations and other help must not be a condition of receiving food.

## Questions to settle before PDF revision

- Define life-giving as meaningful benefit and faithful formation, not mere participation, activity, networking, or personal enjoyment. Opportunities require evidence of what people actually experience.
- Distinguish no direct benefit from harmful depletion. The former may be appropriate to a ministry's focus; the latter needs attention even when other groups benefit.
- The Pastor can benefit from supported, shared leadership without personally operating the pantry. Being the only staff member does not automatically make involvement life giving.
- Pastor and Staff can overlap; churches without separate Staff need a not-applicable option rather than an invented benefit or a penalty.
- Three-of-five is a proposed administrative rule, not a biblical requirement or validated cutoff. Decide whether it is a firm continuation condition, planning aim, or trigger for deeper review.
- A ministry concentrated on one or two groups may still be a faithful response to Christ's calling. A three-group benefit does not justify harmful practices or chronic unsupported depletion elsewhere.
- Preserve evidence of life, hidden cost, and needed support alongside any count. Do not make the goal accumulating checkmarks.

## Biblical context for discussion

Jesus attends to His disciples' need for rest and to the crowd's hunger in [Mark 6:30-44](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+6%3A30-44&version=NIV%3BNKJV). In [Acts 6:1-7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+6%3A1+-+Acts+6%3A7&version=NIV), the church addresses neglected widows through shared responsibility while protecting the apostles' attention to prayer and the ministry of the word. Our application is that the people served and those carrying the work both matter; neither passage prescribes a three-of-five count.

Possible worksheet treatment for discussion: five rows with concrete evidence, direct/indirect benefit, no identified benefit or not-applicable, cost/strain, and next adjustment. Keep harm distinct from neutral/non-direct reach. Three-of-five could prompt a conversation about reach rather than function as automatic permission to continue or shut down a ministry.
