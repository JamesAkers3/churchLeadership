## What changed

The dominant green panel now contains the exact NIV wording of Mark 3:14:

> “He appointed twelve that they might be with him and that he might send them out to preach”

Label: **MARK 3:14 / NIV**. This is verse 14 of a sentence that continues into verse 15, not a complete sentence or a spoken quotation from Jesus. No period was added inside the quotation; NIV's lowercase pronouns are preserved. Checked against [Mark 3:14, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%203%3A14&version=NIV), and the appointment narrative in the [full chapter](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%203&version=NIV). The old "before He trusted them to carry the work" inference is removed. Being with Jesus and being sent are both purposes of the appointment; this passage does not establish a qualification period before He could trust them.

The shared-life path remains. **PUTTING THIS INTO PRACTICE** introduces our own application, with **READ** before the references so the prompts are not presented as Scripture. New prompts:

- With Him: Invite someone to serve beside you. Explain what you're doing and why.
- Sent Together: Agree on a clear responsibility. Give them room to lead, with support.
- Back With Him: Make time to listen afterward. Talk about what happened, and make room for rest.

Read [Mark 6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%206&version=NIV), particularly verses 7-13 and 30-34. The Twelve were sent two by two with authority and instructions, then reported to Jesus; He invited them to rest. Our present-day prompts are an application of this account, not His quoted instructions or a universal mandated staffing process. Jesus remains the One being followed; people are not simply extra capacity for a Pastor. Rest is not conditional on successful reporting, and the account does not promise that all demands disappear.

The three percentages retain equal columns, 34-point Georgia Bold and the original palette/positions. Their verbs now use past tense. The footer identifies all three as historical findings from 508 U.S. Protestant senior Pastors surveyed July 25-August 13, 2019, published May 6, 2020. Primary source: [Barna, Pastors Prefer Lay-Led Initiatives to New Church Programs But Struggle to Develop Leaders](https://www.barna.com/research/inspire-people-to-action/). The 96% combines 68% strong and 28% somewhat agreement. The article directly reports 41% poor leadership development and 9% strong agreement about developing leaders; it also reports 50% somewhat agreement. Do not imply that the remaining 91% all judged development poor, that the three figures form a funnel, or that they establish a cause of burnout. The footer explicitly says these findings do not measure how much ministry a Pastor carries alone. The unrelated 2026 burnout citation was removed from this page.

The opening question, large practical question, action invitation, exhaustion reminder and James' protected Holy Spirit statement are unchanged. Only the small application label is added to the layout. No version appears on the page. No illustration, hyperlink, new statistic or additional assignment was added.
