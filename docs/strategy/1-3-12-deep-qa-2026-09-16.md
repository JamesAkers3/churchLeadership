## Visual-design QA

No clipping, collisions, missing glyphs, or alignment blocker was visible. Major issues:

1. The 115-point, full-width forest-green field becomes a visual stop. It feels like a formal announcement between the personal opening and the relational columns. White-text contrast is strong; the problem is mass and emotional tone. Grayscale intensifies the effect.
2. The 48-point numbers arrive before an 8.7-point muted explanation of non-mandatory headcounts. A hurried reader can retain a quota despite accurate qualifications.
3. Two large closing questions outweigh the small actual instruction to begin one conversation. The action deserves clearer priority.

Minor concerns: the question-to-reference gap is generous; references and the numerical explanation become secondary at reading size; centered explanatory prose is harder to skim than short centered questions. Number/column alignment is good. The greater width of 12 is natural and should not be distorted.

Recommended treatment: pale sage field, dark green heading and body, modestly reduced height (roughly 15-20%) without shrinking supporting type. Alternatives are a distinctly shallower dark band or an open cream field with a restrained accent. Preserve the title, humane story, shared discipleship, Holy Spirit, equal columns, warm palette, and one conversation.

Preserve the restrained typography, Christ/Holy Spirit dependence, shared discipleship, relational labels, and modest first conversation. The potential competing thesis is an unintended numerical staffing system, not the intended biblical relationship reflection.

3. Raise the 7.2-point column references toward 8.5-9 points and darken them. The 7.5-point consolidated footer is also small. Relevant nearby references have greater value than a duplicated comprehensive list if space becomes tight.

Editorial options, not automatic replacements:

## Theological QA

No clear doctrinal error was found. The strongest concern is integrating the Christ-centered statement into the actual formation question, so the green field does not do all the theological work.

1. The 12 question leaves the object of "carry" unspecified. Jesus sent disciples with His received authority and mission, not simply to relieve an organizational leader. Suggested minimal wording: "Who is learning to follow Jesus beside you, and what ministry could you help them carry?" The heading "People you're forming" is permissible human teaching/equipping language and need not be changed on doctrinal grounds.
2. Mark 14:33-34 supports proximity, disclosed sorrow, and requested watchfulness, not proof of an effective three-person support system. The following verses show failure. Broaden the displayed reference to Mark 14:32-42, and protect that context in the leaders guide. Galatians 6 supports mutual burden-bearing across the community, not a mandated three-person group. Current copy does not explicitly claim that the Three succeeded.
3. "Then" could imply a clean graduation from learning before ministry. Their sending preceded later misunderstandings and failure. Options: "He also" or "While they were still learning, He entrusted them with real ministry."
4. Optional closing direction: "Who could you invite to follow Jesus alongside you?" Nearness to the Pastor is appropriate but should serve following Jesus.

The reviewer checked contextual John 15:5; 1 Timothy 4:16; Mark 3:14-15; 5:37; 6:7, 30-31; 9:2; 14:33-34; Galatians 6; and Acts 1:8. No claim of prescribed numbers, rank, or guaranteed growth is present. Do not add a dense doctrinal checklist.

## Church-advocate QA

The central theology is strong. The highest-value pastoral correction is separating receiving support from assigning ministry.

1. "Who could you invite closer?" followed by "What could you begin entrusting to them?" can imply that a safe prayer companion must become a ministry worker. Outside-church support may never appropriately receive local ministry responsibility. Nearness also does not establish readiness, consent, or trust. Keep the first question and one-conversation action; remove the second or explicitly pace it by readiness. Entrusting already belongs in 12 and the opening.
2. "Before considering who you lead" can sound like a disqualifying prerequisite to a guilt-burdened Pastor. Suggested option: "As you lead, what is helping you remain in Jesus?"
3. Repeated passive formation language can leave Pastor-as-maker and congregation-as-material. It is not intrinsically objectifying, and the shared-disciple statement corrects it. Optional heading "People learning beside you" or a shared-walk sentence could help; one adjustment may suffice. Root should not automatically replace the approved heading or flatten formation into generic peer support.
4. "Ready to carry" can sound like offloading work; connect the question with calling, gifts, or serving together without adding a long qualification list.
5. Make existing numerical safeguards easier to notice.

What passes: no growth/performance promise; no secrecy/unconditional loyalty; honest prayer and outside-church support are legitimate; mistakes are part of learning; one conversation is workable for a solo Pastor or small church. This is more useful than simply saying "disciple people": it joins the Pastor's dependence and support with relational learning and eventual entrusted ministry.

## Voice / editorial QA

The title and opening sound warm, human, and grounded. The thesis is coherent. The reviewer preserved approved language rather than treating every polished sentence as suspect. Exact priorities:

1. "Helping them become ready to carry" is awkward and leaves the burden unspecified. Suggested alternative: "How are you helping them prepare to serve?" This must be integrated with theology's requirement to retain Jesus-directed formation and entrusted ministry rather than generic service alone.
2. "Before considering who you lead" is formal and mildly corrective. Suggested alternative: "As you lead others."
3. "The Pastor and the people being formed" categorizes people institutionally and passively despite valuable theology. Optional conversational version: "Pastor, we are all disciples of Jesus." The shared-disciple meaning must not be lost; the existing wording is not a theological error and need not change automatically.
4. "Pray. Put a name to it. Begin with one conversation" has a slightly campaign-like clipped cadence, and "it" is vague. Suggested alternative: "Pray about who to invite closer. Begin with one conversation."
5. "See beyond the demands of ministry" is abstract. Optional alternative: "Know you beyond your ministry."

The green field feels like a proclamation plaque because of its mass and typography, not because its theology is inappropriate. Preserve "Christ at the center" and "We remain in Him and depend on the Holy Spirit." Adding "to guide us" is an optional approval point, not a necessary correction.

Voice QA also identifies the split between invite and entrust; keep one conversation unmistakably the immediate action. Preserve the humane opening, own-walk/trust labels, learning-beside-you idea, numerical safeguards, outside-church support, and Pastor capitalization.

No reliable AI-authorship conclusion is claimed. These are observable editorial characteristics: passive categories, vague burden language, formal framing, and clipped cadence.
