# Pastoral Support and 1–3–12 Split: Decision and Final QA

## Why the original page was split

The original `christ-at-the-center-1-3-12.pdf` contained strong individual ideas but asked one page to carry two different arguments:

1. Pastors can be visible or personally known while still lacking meaningful support.
2. Jesus' relationships with the Three and the Twelve give us a useful pattern for examining a leader's relationships.

The statistics, concentric-circle framework, explanatory copy, and several reflection questions competed for attention. The deepest questions on the page felt like a third message instead of one clear next step. A pastor reviewing the page correctly identified that the material should become two PDFs.

The original combined page is preserved as `output/pdf/archive/christ-at-the-center-1-3-12-v1.pdf`.

## The two focused pages

### A Pastor Can Be Known and Still Unsupported

One argument: being recognized or known does not guarantee dependable spiritual support.

One next step: begin with one safe, honest conversation.

### Christ at the Center — 1–3–12

One argument: Christ remains the source, and healthy relationships help tend the leader's soul, share weight, and form people without turning the circles into rank or a required headcount.

One next step: put names to the circles, write down one name, and begin there.

## What the earlier QA process missed

The earlier reviews were too compartmentalized. They found issues within theology, typography, pastoral tone, and individual statements, but they did not require every reviewer to test the page as a complete communication. A page can contain accurate theology, strong statistics, good design, and helpful questions while still failing to make one coherent argument.

## QA standard going forward

Every visual must pass both its specialist review and these shared questions:

1. What is the one argument this page makes?
2. What is the one next step it asks the reader to take?
3. Does any element introduce a competing thesis?
4. Could a pastor misunderstand the page, feel blamed, or hear pressure to try harder?
5. Is Jesus central to the practice itself, or merely attached to it afterward?
6. Can the page stand on its own without a facilitator explaining away confusion?

The final split pages received fresh reviews from theological, church-advocate, visual-design, voice, digital-art, and publishing perspectives. The final wording also received a targeted recheck after the voice audit removed the last corporate or formulaic phrases.
