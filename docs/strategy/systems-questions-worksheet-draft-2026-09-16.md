## Page 1: Why are we doing this?

Pastor, you do not have to carry this conversation alone or arrive with every answer. Bring the people who do the work and the people affected by it. With Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, ask honestly what should continue, what needs care, and what needs to change.

Identify the ministry/process/system, church, date, and people in the conversation. Record observations next to each question.

| What we examine | Questions worth asking |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Why does this exist? What biblical responsibility or real need is it serving? |
| Vision | What do we hope will be different in people's lives because of this? |
| Mission | How does this help us practice our mission - not just repeat our mission statement? |
| Christ at the center | Does the way we do this reflect Jesus' character and methods? What in Scripture supports - or challenges - our approach? |
| Fruit | What is actually happening? What have the people involved experienced, beyond attendance or activity? |
| Honest examination | If we were starting today, would we build it this way? Why? |
| Hidden cost | Who is carrying more than we realize? What does this require in time, money, relationships, and strength? |
| Hard conversations | What concern have we avoided naming? What makes it difficult to talk about? |

## Page 2: Is it life giving?

Systems do not create life. Jesus does. Healthy systems make room for the life He is already growing.

For each area, record life and fruit, cost and strain, and needed changes.

| Who it touches | Question |
|---|---|
| The Pastor | Does this support faithful, shared leadership - or depend on the Pastor continually rescuing it? |
| The congregation | Are people being formed, equipped, and entrusted - or repeatedly asked to fill gaps? |
| The community | Have we listened to the people we hope to serve? Are we responding to their actual needs? |
| The world through missions | How does our church's ministry cultivate prayer, generosity, partnership, and faithful service beyond our community? |

Life-giving does not mean effortless. Slow or costly ministry is not automatically failing. Every system does not need to serve all four directions equally. Consider its particular calling, and the church's ministry as a whole.

## Page 3: What will we do next?

With Scripture open and the Holy Spirit leading, decide together what should change and how you will carry it. Choose one step you can put into practice and review.

Consider: Strengthen, Share, Simplify, Redesign, Pause, Release.

- What did we decide, and why?
- What is our one next faithful step?
- Who will carry it?
- What authority, training, resources, and support will they need?
- What biblical responsibility must remain, even if we change how we carry it?
- Who needs to be heard before we act?
- How will we know it is helping? Notice both fruit and cost. Listen to the people involved.
- When will we review it, and who will be there?

You do not have to repair every system at once. Begin with what needs your attention, support the people carrying it, and return to learn together.

## Reference and theological continuity

The separation of purpose, vision, and mission is informed by [Don't Panic London's Brand Purpose vs. Mission vs. Vision guide](https://www.dontpaniclondon.com/blog/brand-vision-mission-purpose/), especially its at-a-glance comparison and decision test. It was revisited September 16, 2026. Its comparison image could not be retrieved in the research tool on that visit; the article's written definitions and explanations were accessible. No proprietary table or business examples were copied into this worksheet. The questions are rewritten for our use case, with systems, pastoral health, Life-Giving Reach, and shared practical discernment drawn from our project frameworks.

Scripture remains the standard, not the branding article. The church's biblical calling is received from Christ rather than invented in a workshop. Existing grounding is in `docs/strategy/posture-of-the-work.md`, including John 16:12-15; Mark 7:6-13; Matthew 23:1-12, 23-28; and Acts 15:1-29. The gift-and-empowerment foundation remains 2 Timothy 1:6-7. These references are interpretive context, not claims that Scripture prescribes this worksheet's format. Do not use efficiency or visible growth as proof of faithfulness, or lack of measurable growth alone as a reason to end biblical responsibilities.

The participant PDF has no website links, visible version labels, Barna statistics, or borrowed quotations. Reference notes stay here for continuity.
