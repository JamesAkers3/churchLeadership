## Best fit: needs Pastors themselves identify

[Barna: What Relieves Pastoral Burnout - July 6, 2026](https://www.barna.com/research/what-relieves-pastoral-burnout/)

The article reports areas where Pastors say they could use the most help:

| Finding | Safe description | Do not substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 52% | Identify mental and physical health as an area where they most need help. | 52% have a mental illness, are unhealthy, have PTSD or are clinically burned out. |
| 41% | Identify close, supportive relationships as an area where they most need help. | 41% have no friends or have no support at all. |
| 36% | Identify financial stability as an area where they most need help. | 36% are poor, insolvent or underpaid. |

Population: **507 U.S. Protestant senior Pastors**. Survey fieldwork: **January 29-February 8, 2026**, Barna with Gloo. Publication date is not fieldwork date. These are reported needs, not clinical measurements. Do not add percentages or portray them as exclusive groups, a pie chart or stages of one process. The public article does not publish the complete questionnaire or a subgroup cross-tab proving who experiences each combination.

**Recommendation:** use 52% and 41% as two equally sized figures under the existing reassurance heading. Their relevance is that receiving help with health or relationships is a need other ministry leaders name, too. Financial stability belongs in later delivery notes and church-leader conversations about material support, not necessarily a third visual number.

The article also places extended rest, shared responsibility and role adjustments among actions respondents expect to be especially helpful but difficult to access. This supports discussing what leaders can make possible. It does not demonstrate a clinical treatment effect or promise that delegation resolves every individual's distress. Printed statistics require source, year, population and n; application language remains ours.

## Additional candidates and their limitations

### Current exhaustion finding: valid, but different purpose

[Barna: Pastors' Sense of Calling Is Up - May 5, 2026](https://www.barna.com/research/pastors-calling-satisfaction-research-2026/)

The public article reports just over 60% feeling emotionally or mentally exhausted frequently or sometimes; 2026 n=507 U.S. senior Protestant Pastors. The current "more than 6 in 10" remains a valid approximate restatement. It recognizes an experience; the proposed 52%/41% pair identifies desired help. Neither is a stronger clinical measure. The published trend improves over the decade; do not use older, larger exhaustion percentages to imply a worsening 2026 crisis.

### Professional support: useful in the guide, avoid blame

[Barna: 5 Opportunities to Better Support Pastors - July 15, 2025](https://www.barna.com/trends/support-pastors/)

The article describes more than half not currently using professional support or guidance. Its category includes mentors, advisors, coaches and counselors, not only mental-health treatment. The article lists 2024 research with 551 U.S. Protestant senior Pastors, September 10-17, 2024, quotas and weighting, reported sample error +/-3.8 at 95% confidence. Because the narrative gives an approximation, do not invent an exact overall percentage. Non-use does not establish fear of asking, refusal, pride, lack of access or absence of family/peer support. Prefer the needs pair for this visual; use this finding carefully in facilitator notes.

### Rest and Sabbath: leader preparation, not causal proof

[Barna: How Rest & Sabbath Can Strengthen Pastoral Well-Being - June 15, 2023](https://www.barna.com/research/rest-sabbath/)

Useful for planning consistent leave practices and coverage. Its research includes 2015 n=901 and September 2022 n=585 U.S. Protestant senior Pastors. Associations between self-care and well-being are not proof that a sabbatical cures burnout or trauma. Do not frame needed leave as punishment, a forced exit or a reward earned by performance. Keep operational application distinct from survey findings and individual clinical guidance.

### Self-harm/suicide: not a headline for this visual

[Barna: Pastors, Too, Grapple with Thoughts of Suicide, Self-Harm - September 12, 2024](https://www.barna.com/research/pastors-thoughts-suicide-self-harm/)

Reviewed for safeguarding context, not proposed for the Pastor-facing slide. The self-reported combined question covers thoughts of self-harm or suicide in the past year, with varying severity; 2023 n=523 U.S. Protestant senior Pastors, August 28-September 18. Do not equate the combined measure with suicide intent or attempts, or assert a time trend for a newly asked question. It could dominate a reassurance page and needs deliberate expert-reviewed handling. The talking sheet's urgent-support guidance remains important without making a crisis percentage the chapter's emotional centerpiece.
