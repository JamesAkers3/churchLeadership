# Open Work and Next Steps

This is the bridge between what we have learned and what we still need to build.

## From Diagnosis to Practice

> **We have learned how to name what is happening. Now we need to help churches know what to do next.**

We have done meaningful work to identify the pressures pastors and churches are facing. We have named scattered passion, unclear vision, divided direction, unsupported leadership, gaps in formation, systems that drain life, disconnection from the community, and the slow depletion that can lead toward burnout.

Naming those things matters. It can bring relief when a pastor finally has words for what he or she has been carrying. But diagnosis cannot be the finish line.

The next phase must turn what we are seeing into simple, Christ-centered, life-giving systems that a church can practice, evaluate, and sustain. These systems are not replacements for prayer, discernment, dependence on the Holy Spirit, or relationships. They should make room for those things instead of crowding them out.

> **Systems do not create life. Jesus does. Healthy systems make room for the life He is already growing.**

## The Questions Every Framework Must Answer

Every framework, assessment, worksheet, and recommendation should help a church answer five questions:

1. **What are we seeing?**

   Name what is actually happening without shame, exaggeration, or pretending.

2. **Why might it be happening?**

   Look beneath the symptom. Listen to the pastor, the people, the history, and the community before reaching a conclusion.

3. **What does Jesus show us?**

   Examine His character, priorities, relationships, questions, instructions, boundaries, and methods. Scripture is not decoration added after the strategy is built. Jesus is the standard from the beginning.

4. **What life-giving system could address it?**

   Build the smallest faithful rhythm, process, or structure that can help people carry the work together.

5. **How will we practice, evaluate, and refine it?**

   Put it into use, pay attention to its fruit and its cost, listen to the people carrying it, and make honest adjustments.

## The Working Rhythm

**Listen → Discern → Design → Practice → Evaluate → Refine**

### Listen

Begin with people, not assumptions. Hear the pastor, staff, board, congregation, and community. Learn what they love, what they are carrying, where they have been wounded, and where life is already showing up.

### Discern

Pray, examine Scripture, ask better questions, and separate the visible symptom from the deeper issue. Do not rush to copy what worked somewhere else.

### Design

Create a simple response that fits this church, this community, this season, and the people God has already placed there. Give the system a clear purpose, owner, boundary, and next step.

### Practice

Start small enough to learn. Equip people, entrust something real, and create a safe place for questions, mistakes, encouragement, and correction.

### Evaluate

Ask whether the system is helping people become healthier, share responsibility, remain connected to Christ, serve others well, and participate in the mission more faithfully. Activity alone is not fruit.

### Refine

Keep what gives life. Repair what is unclear. Release what continues to drain people without serving the calling. Then begin the rhythm again by listening.

## Systems We Still Need to Build

### 1. Leadership Formation and Shared Ministry

**What we have named:** Pastors doing too much themselves, unsupported leadership, and the gap between believing ministry should be shared and intentionally forming people to carry it.

**What still needs to be built:** A practical leadership-development and delegation pathway rooted in the way Jesus brought people close, formed them, entrusted real responsibility, sent them together, received them back, and continued developing them.

This should include:

- A 1–3–12 relationship assessment
- A gifts, readiness, and growth conversation
- A watch → help → carry → equip rhythm
- Clear assignments and healthy authority
- Return, reflection, correction, encouragement, and rest
- A multiplication pathway that does not treat people as staffing solutions

### 2. Pastor Care and Shared Weight

**What we have named:** Burnout, isolation, compassion fatigue, repeated crisis exposure, and pastors who may still have passion but no longer have the capacity to carry everything being placed on them.

**What still needs to be built:** A pastor-care and shared-weight system that helps a church respond before exhaustion becomes collapse.

This should include:

- Regular whole-person check-ins
- Trusted relationships inside and outside the church
- Clear confidentiality and referral boundaries
- Board and elder responsibility for pastoral health
- Crisis load-sharing and recovery practices
- Rest that is protected rather than merely encouraged
- Safe pathways to qualified medical or mental-health care when needed

### 3. Vision, Mission, and Focus

**What we have named:** Passion spread across too many priorities, unclear vision, divided direction, and energy that never consistently reaches the place God has called the church to serve.

**What still needs to be built:** A Christ-centered vision and mission process that creates a shared direction and becomes a real decision-making tool.

This should include:

- Prayerful listening and Scriptural grounding
- A clear picture of the people and place being served
- Vision and mission development in plain language
- A ministry alignment filter
- A way to say no without shame or defensiveness
- A regular rhythm for returning to the calling and adjusting the work

### 4. Life-Giving Systems Audit

**What we have named:** Churches becoming victims of the very systems meant to help them, resources continuing to flow toward work that is no longer fruitful, and staff carrying processes no one has stopped to question.

**What still needs to be built:** A practical systems audit centered on one honest question:

> **Is it life giving?**

This should help leaders examine:

- Why the system exists
- Who it serves
- Who carries it and what it costs them
- Whether it supports the church's mission
- What fruit or harm is actually visible
- Whether to strengthen, simplify, redesign, pause, or release it

### 5. Community Listening and Engagement

**What we have named:** Churches that care deeply about their communities but struggle to establish relationships, understand what people are carrying, or sustain meaningful engagement.

**What still needs to be built:** A community listening and engagement framework that can be adapted to different neighborhoods, histories, needs, cultures, and church capacities.

This should include:

- Listening before planning
- Mapping trusted local voices, relationships, assets, needs, and wounds
- Recognizing where the church is already present
- Partnership discernment
- A small, relational first step
- Measures of trust, faithfulness, and sustained presence—not merely attendance

### 6. Church Image, Communication, and First Impressions

**What we have named:** Confusing public identity, inconsistent visual presentation, weak or outdated internet presence, unclear social communication, and a gap between who the church believes it is and what a visitor experiences.

**What still needs to be built:** A complete outsider-view assessment with a practical improvement pathway.

This should include:

- Website and search presence
- Social media and public communication
- Visual consistency and branding
- Physical arrival and first impressions
- Clarity for guests and the surrounding community
- Alignment between public promises and the lived experience of the church

### 7. Healthy Use of AI

**What we have named:** Churches need help using AI wisely without allowing efficiency, imitation, or convenience to enter places that require prayer, presence, pastoral responsibility, and the work of the Holy Spirit.

**What still needs to be built:** A healthy AI framework with clear uses, guardrails, review practices, and sacred boundaries.

This should include:

- Appropriate administrative and creative uses
- Human review and accountability
- Privacy, confidentiality, and data boundaries
- Disclosure and integrity
- Areas where AI can assist but must not replace human presence
- Areas that should remain distinctly pastoral, relational, prayerful, and sacred

### 8. Evaluation and Learning

**What we have named:** Churches may continue activities because they are familiar, visible, or difficult to stop, while lacking a healthy way to learn what is bearing fruit and what is draining people.

**What still needs to be built:** A simple evaluation rhythm that helps leaders learn without turning ministry into a scorecard.

This should include:

- A clear purpose for every ministry or system
- A few meaningful signs of health and faithfulness
- Stories and human experience alongside numbers
- The cost to the people carrying the work
- Regular review conversations
- Permission to refine, pause, or release work without treating change as failure

## Foundation for This Endeavor

Before we ask churches to clarify their foundation, we need to keep building our own:

1. Continue refining the Scriptural foundation, with 2 Timothy 1:6–7 as the primary anchor.
2. Write James' mission.
3. Write James' vision.
4. Clarify the promise and boundaries of the work.
5. Establish the brand: name, colors, typography, logo, email signature, and visual language.
6. Design the service journey from first conversation through evaluation and follow-through.
7. Determine pricing, pilot engagements, agreements, and the basic administration needed to serve churches well.

This is not starting over. Jesus called the disciples to be with Him before He sent them. We are making sure the work has roots before asking it to bear more weight.
